\paragraph*{General Limiting Distribution with Weak Instruments and Weak Exogeneity.}

Assume now we instead imposed a weaker assumption that \Cref{ass:exog}. In particular, consider the following assumption.
\begin{assumptionp}{IVW}[Weak Exogeneity]\label{ass:weak-exog}
    \(\E[Z_iU_i]=0_{d_Z}\).
\end{assumptionp}

% In the following, we derive the limiting distribution of the TLR statistic without imposing strong exogeneity, only weak exogeneity. We record the assumption as follows.

\noindent
Under the weaker assumption, \Cref{lem:bnh} does not obtain. It thus follows that we obtain a less tractable version of \Cref{prop:wi}.

% We now obtain the following result.

\begin{propositionp}{WI}[Weak-instrument Limiting Distribution]
\label{prop:wi}
    Under \Cref{ass:weak-exog}, the limiting distribution of the TLR statistic with weak instruments is given by.
    \begin{align}
    LR(\beta_0) \convd \sum_{j=1}^{2d_Z}  W_j^2\left(\frac{\lambda^*  \kappa_j}{1+\lambda^* \kappa_j}\right)^2 \qquad \text{s.t.} \qquad \sum_{j=1}^{2d_Z}\kappa_j w_j^2 = 0
\end{align}
where \(W\sim \mathfrak{N}(w,\boldsymbol{\iota}_{2d_Z})\) and \(W_j\) are the components of this vector.
\end{propositionp}
\begin{proof}
    See Appendix X.
\end{proof}

Without any further structure, this problem has \(4d_Z-1\) nuisance parameters. We have consistent estimators for all \(\kappa_j\). However, for \(w\) we only have the \(2d_Z\)-dimensional statistic \(\hat W\). It is possible to perform a version of the TLR-BB test.
